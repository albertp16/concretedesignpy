RC Column Concrete Jacketing

TN-RET-001 — ACI 318-19, ACI 562-16, ASCE 41-17, Mander et al. (1988) via fib Bulletin 14

Existing Column (as measured, not as drawn)

Feed this from a condition assessment. ASCE 41-17 Table 10-1 multiplies lower-bound f'c by 1.50 and fy by 1.25 for expected values — decide which you are passing and stay consistent across the whole form.

Jacket

A partial jacket computes its geometry, axial strength and P-M interaction only. Confinement, shear, interface, stiffness and detailing have no model for it and are reported unavailable rather than estimated, and P·e is not added to Mu. Two adjacent faces are refused outright — that section's capacity is a biaxial surface, not a curve.

Bars restrained per face drives the Mander kₑ and is the input most often set wrong. A plain perimeter hoop restrains only the four corner bars — that is 2. Restraining every bar requires crossties (ACI 318-19 25.7.2.3); assuming full restraint when the detail is a plain hoop overstates f'cc by roughly 12%.

Factored Demand on the Jacketed Column

After jacketing the column is several times stiffer and attracts more force. If these came from a model of the un-jacketed structure, every result below is optimistic.

Construction

The single most consequential field. A jacket stopping at the slab soffit must push its entire axial share across the interface; one bearing on the foundation transfers it by direct bearing and the axial term vanishes.
